LEAD:

Britain starts repairing the joins in its crypto rulebook [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made].

The United Kingdom's Cryptoassets Miscellaneous Amendments Regulations took effect on 20 November 2026, ahead of the wider regime's full start on 25 October 2027 [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made]. The instrument changes the treatment of qualifying stablecoin backing and adjusts the perimeter around several crypto activities [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made].

The new instrument is a small statute with a large task: make the planned rulebook fit the firms and assets that will have to use it [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made]. It amends the Regulated Activities Order, the Financial Promotion Order, and the 2026 Cryptoassets Regulations [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made]. Its explanatory note says the changes include exclusions for proprietary trading, market making, and mere technical services, plus provisions on safeguarding stablecoin backing assets [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made].

The immediate legal result is not a general licence for crypto business [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made]. Rather, the regulations move specified provisions concerning collective-investment schemes, electronic money, and alternative-investment-fund managers into force 21 days after the amendments themselves [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348287233/regulation/4/data.html]. The framework still defines a qualifying stablecoin as a qualifying cryptoasset that seeks to maintain a stable value against a fiat currency and is supported by fiat or other assets held for that purpose [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/part/III/2026-02-25/data.htm].

That detail is the point of the day's event [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made]. Stablecoin reserves, technical providers, and liquidity firms sit at the boundaries where a broad statutory category can catch activity that legislators did not mean to regulate [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made]. Parliament has now published its answer for those boundaries, while the main regime remains scheduled for 25 October 2027 [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made].

It moves those questions from consultation into enacted text [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/contents/made].

NEWS

[NEWS] Binance says it has covered assets affected by its security incident [https://www.binance.com/en-AE/square/post/387767278467020].

Binance said on 13 November 2026 that it had completed coverage of assets affected by the incident and had committed more than $6.34 billion of company funds to cover losses [https://www.binance.com/en-AE/square/post/387767278467020]. The company said it funded the response with part of its Secure Asset Fund for Users, part of its futures insurance fund, and corporate treasury assets [https://www.binance.com/en-AE/square/post/387767278467020]. Its proof-of-reserves snapshot from 12 November 2026 covers 198 tokens, although Binance said the associated zero-knowledge proof would take about 40 to 50 hours to generate [https://www.binance.com/en-AE/square/post/387767278467020].

[NEWS] DBS and Circle open a direct USDC channel for eligible institutions [https://www.circle.com/pressroom/dbs-and-circle-expand-institutional-access-to-usdc-in-asia].

DBS and Circle announced on 18 November 2026 that eligible institutional clients could mint and redeem USDC directly through DBS [https://www.circle.com/pressroom/dbs-and-circle-expand-institutional-access-to-usdc-in-asia]. The companies said the arrangement supports round-the-clock USD-USDC settlement rather than dependence on secondary-market liquidity [https://www.circle.com/pressroom/dbs-and-circle-expand-institutional-access-to-usdc-in-asia]. The announcement is a company statement, not evidence of client uptake or transaction volume [https://www.circle.com/pressroom/dbs-and-circle-expand-institutional-access-to-usdc-in-asia].

[NEWS] Coinbase disables two token markets after its listing review [https://status.exchange.coinbase.com/].

Coinbase reported on 20 November 2026 that it had disabled trading for Stargate Finance and Vulcan Forged after a listing-standards review [https://status.exchange.coinbase.com/]. It said the STG and PYR order books had first moved to limit-only mode, where customers could place and cancel limit orders but could not submit market orders [https://status.exchange.coinbase.com/]. Coinbase said the assets remained withdrawable, a distinction between a trading suspension and custody access [https://status.exchange.coinbase.com/].

TECH

[TECH] Ethereum's frame-transaction draft puts validation and payment inside the transaction [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141].

EIP-8141 proposes a transaction made of frames that can validate a transaction, approve gas payment, and execute user operations [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141]. The draft's public-mempool rules require nodes to simulate the validation prefix and reject transactions that, among other things, exceed the verification-gas limit or read storage outside the sender [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141]. The specification remains a draft; discussion activity on 20 November 2026 is not an activation decision [https://ethereum-magicians.org/c/eips/eips-core/35].

[TECH] A draft registry would let validators publish post-quantum keys before using them [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8450-post-quantum-validator-key-registry/30046].

EIP-8450 proposes a beacon-state registry in which a validator registers a leanXMSS public key against its existing validator index [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8450-post-quantum-validator-key-registry/30046]. The proposal says the key would remain dormant until a later fork assigned duties to it, making registration a preparation step rather than a change to current validator duties [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8450-post-quantum-validator-key-registry/30046]. One commenter asked whether a registered key should be able to authorize its successor without an execution-layer call, an unresolved design question rather than a settled feature [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8450-post-quantum-validator-key-registry/30046].

CULTURE

[CULTURE] Coin Center asks the SEC to separate software authors from tokenized venues [https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-to-the-securities-and-exchange-commission-on-order-granting-temporary-conditional-exemptive-relief/].

Coin Center's 17 November 2026 comment on the SEC's temporary Innovation Exemption welcomes the agency's effort while asking for formal limits on jurisdiction over software publication and neutral blockchain infrastructure [https://coincenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/coin-center-sec-innovation-exemption-comment-2026-11-17.pdf]. The group also asks the SEC to say that an independent developer does not become part of a tokenized-securities venue merely because that venue adopts published software [https://coincenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/coin-center-sec-innovation-exemption-comment-2026-11-17.pdf]. This is an advocate's position in a live rulemaking argument, not a statement of SEC policy [https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-to-the-securities-and-exchange-commission-on-order-granting-temporary-conditional-exemptive-relief/].

[CULTURE] Ethlabs argues that Ethereum should make post-quantum work its 2027 priority [https://ethlabs.org/writings/pqai27.html].

Ethlabs wrote on 11 November 2026 that Ethereum should aim to become post-quantum and plausibly post-AI in 2027 [https://ethlabs.org/writings/pqai27.html]. The authors argue that work not on that path, including some prospective Hegotá work, should be reconsidered [https://ethlabs.org/writings/pqai27.html]. That is a roadmap argument from a participant in the debate, not an Ethereum protocol decision [https://ethlabs.org/writings/pqai27.html].
